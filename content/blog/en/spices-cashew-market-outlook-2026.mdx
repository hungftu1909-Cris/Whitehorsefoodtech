## Spices: steady growth, black pepper leading by share

According to MarketDataForecast, the global spices market reached **US$27.51 billion in 2025** and is projected to grow to **US$28.95 billion in 2026**, with a compound annual growth rate of roughly **5.25%** through 2034. Notably, **black pepper is forecast to account for 33.39% of the entire spice market in 2026** — the largest share of any single spice — driven by its distinctive flavor and growing awareness of its health benefits. Cinnamon holds roughly 6–7% share by volume, while star anise and other aromatic spices also contribute meaningfully to overall growth.

## Cashew: 4–7% annual growth depending on the report

Cashew market reports show a fairly wide 2026 forecast range — from roughly $3.57 billion to over $10 billion depending on scope, with growth rates ranging from **4.25% to 6.74% per year**. Key drivers: rising global nut consumption overall, the expanding plant-based food trend, and growing demand for roasted and flavored cashew varieties.

## A shared consumer trend across both categories

Health-conscious consumers are driving demand for turmeric, ginger, cinnamon and black pepper for their perceived health benefits. At the same time, buyers — both end consumers and importing businesses — are increasingly looking for spices sourced through **ethical procurement, environmentally responsible farming, and transparent supply chains**. This trend lines up directly with the traceability focus that Vietnamese agri exporters, including Whitehorse Foodtech, are building around.

## What this means for importers

With black pepper holding the largest share of the spice market and cashew demand continuing to grow, these remain two commodities worth building long-term supply relationships around rather than one-off purchases. Choosing a supplier that can demonstrate clear origin and quality standards will matter more as end buyers demand greater transparency.
